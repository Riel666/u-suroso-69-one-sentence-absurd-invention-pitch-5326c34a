One absurd invention pitch, 130 characters: a TV remote that solves the ancient problem of vanishing down the sofa by screaming for help in your mother's voice.

The joke is the mechanism itself: the remote does not beep politely or blink a light, it panics in the one voice no one in the house can ignore. The annoyance is tiny and universal — the remote always slips between the cushions — and the fix is silly yet weirdly plausible, a lost gadget that refuses to stay lost quietly.
